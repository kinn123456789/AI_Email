What this module does

When a learner's free trial ends without them enrolling, this module reaches back out — automatically, but never impulsively. Once a day, it checks whose trial reached the 1-day, 3-day, or 7-day mark since expiry, writes a warm, personalized email with AI, and quietly saves it as a draft. Nothing goes out to a real family until a staff member actually opens it, reads it, and clicks Send.

Features

What happens at /trial-followup.

Automatic Daily Check

Once a day, every free trial that has reached its 1/3/7-day follow-up mark is found automatically — no one has to track expiry dates by hand.

AI-Written, Human-Sent

Each email is drafted by AI, but always waits for a staff member's review and click before it ever reaches a real inbox.

Smart Enough to Stop

If a family already enrolled after their trial, the sequence recognizes that and doesn't keep nudging them to sign up.

Dedicated Dashboard

See every draft, what's due, reply to responses, and review completed campaigns — all in one place.

Trash & Restore

Drafts that shouldn't go out can be trashed, and brought back later if needed.

Under the hood

Technical details

Cadence & candidate logic process_trial_followup.py

Runs once a day at 9:00 AM (scheduler.py, CronTrigger(hour=9, minute=0), confirmed directly in the scheduler registration). Each candidate's own progress is compared against trial_expiry_at vs. right now — exact thresholds, traced directly in the code:

  • Email 1: sent once at least 1 day has passed since the trial expired, and email 1 hasn't gone out yet.
  • Email 2: sent once at least 3 days have passed since expiry — but only if email 1 already went out and email 2 hasn't.
  • Email 3: sent once at least 7 days have passed since expiry — but only if emails 1 and 2 already went out and email 3 hasn't; this also marks the whole campaign complete.
  • Anyone who already converted to a paid subscription is skipped entirely, regardless of where they are in the sequence.
  • Each candidate's progress (which emails have already been sent) is tracked in its own record, created the first time that trial is seen.

How "already converted" is actually checked trial_followup.py

"Smart Enough to Stop" isn't detecting a change or an event — it's a simple snapshot check on two timestamps, re-run fresh every single day the job runs, not just once when the campaign starts:

if subscribed_at and start: subscribed_at = isoparse(subscribed_at) if subscribed_at >= start: converted = True
  • start is the trial's own enrollment start timestamp — when this free trial began, not when it expires.
  • subscribed_at is when the learner's currently-active subscription began (subscription_status == "active" is filtered before this check even runs).
  • subscribed_at >= start answers a genuine before/after question — "did the subscription start at or after this trial started?" That's what actually matters for "did this trial lead to a conversion," not an equality check like subscribed_at != expiry, which would almost always be True anyway since two independently-recorded timestamps are essentially never identical down to the second.

Worked example: trial starts July 1, expires July 15, subscription starts July 10 → July 10 >= July 1 → True → correctly counted as converted. If instead the learner had some unrelated old active subscription from back in March → March >= July 1 → False → correctly not counted, since that subscription predates this trial.

Fixed this session This check re-runs fresh every day, so converting mid-sequence correctly stops later emails. Previously nothing wrote back to the learner's campaign row to mark it "converted" — it just silently sat at status="active" forever. Now mark_followup_converted(learner_id) sets status='converted' at the exact point conversion is detected (only on a still-active row) — a distinct outcome from status='completed' (finishing all 3 emails without converting), so the separate "Completed Campaigns" count stays accurate.
Also worth knowing The check only looks for subscription_status == "active" — not "anything other than expired." A learner who subscribed and then had that subscription cancelled or a payment fail would not be caught by this, so they'd still be treated as a valid follow-up candidate and keep getting nudged, even though they did convert at some point.

AI drafting followup_ai.py

  • Each of the 3 emails has its own separately written prompt matching its day's tone (thank-you / nudge / last-reminder).
  • Uses the same name-hiding technique as the Subscription & Re-engagement module: the AI only ever sees generic placeholder tokens for the parent and learner's names while writing, and the real names are swapped back in afterward — meant to stop the AI from ever inventing or mixing up a name.
  • Every attempt (success or failure) is logged for later debugging; a safe fallback template is used if the AI call fails.
Fixed this session Just like the Subscription module, the placeholder swap-back is now checked afterward: if any leftover bracketed placeholder-shaped text is found in the body, the draft is discarded and the safe fallback template is used instead — a stray placeholder can no longer sit unnoticed in a draft shown to staff.

Draft-first by design

Sending is deliberately not automatic anywhere in this flow — every generated email is saved as a draft first, and the actual send only happens when a staff member clicks Send from the dashboard. This was a conscious choice in how the feature was built, not a missing step.

Cleanup note

Two old files in this area (trial_followup_scheduler.py, an earlier standalone scheduler prototype, and trial_email_sender.py, an unused duplicate of the live sending code) are not used by the running app and are safe candidates for removal.
